From NTT DoCoMo to Universal Standard

How 176 monochrome 12x12 pixel icons created in Tokyo transformed into an omnipresent global lingua franca preserved in the Museum of Modern Art.

📟
1999 • Genesis in Tokyo

Shigetaka Kurita

Designed 176 12x12 pixel pictograms for NTT DoCoMo pagers and early mobile internet (i-mode), inspired by weather symbols and manga kanji.

🌐
2010 • Standardization

Unicode 6.0 Integration

Unicode Consortium standardizes 722 emojis, ensuring cross-platform character parity and preventing mojibake (garbled text across telecom networks).

📱
2011 • Smartphone Boom

iOS & Android Keyboards

Apple embeds the emoji keyboard into iOS 5; Google adds native Android support. In 2015, Oxford Dictionaries names 😂 Word of the Year.

🏛️
2016+ • MoMA & Legal Era

Museum of Modern Art

MoMA acquires Kurita's original 176 glyph set for its permanent architecture and design collection. Global courts rule emojis carry binding legal intent.

🎨
The MoMA Design Philosophy
"Emojis are a revolutionary visual shorthand that reintroduces human emotion, tone, and inflection into the sterile realm of digital text."
